# 44 · Building an Item Bank: How IRT Makes Items from Different Batches "Comparable"

> Many organisations want an "item bank" of their own — items reused, tests assembled to order, built up year after year. This article explains why an item bank depends on IRT, and the key craft that makes everything comparable: "equating".

## First, what an "item bank" is

An item bank isn't simply "a folder full of items". A real item bank is a collection of items **each of which carries stable "identity parameters" (difficulty, discrimination, fairness, and so on), all on the same ruler, so that they can be compared with one another**.

With an item bank like that, you can:

- **Assemble tests to order**: want a test of medium difficulty? Pick items of suitable difficulty from the bank and put them together.
- **Use multiple forms to prevent cheating**: produce several different forms while still guaranteeing fairness (because all the items are on one ruler).
- **Track change year on year**: use one batch of items this year and another next year, and the results are still comparable.
- **Run adaptive tests**: the item bank is CAT's arsenal (article 28).

**Without IRT, an item bank can't be built** — because under ordinary scoring an item's difficulty changes with the population (article 2), and there's no such thing as "stable, comparable identity parameters".

## The core difficulty: how to put items from different batches on "the same ruler"

An item bank is built up batch by batch. This year you calibrate 100 items, next year another 100, each with **different people**.

That raises a problem:

- This year's calibration used group A; next year's uses group B.
- If group A is stronger overall than group B, the same notion of difficulty gets "anchored" at different points in the two calibrations.
- Result: **although both batches' parameters are called "difficulty b", they aren't on the same ruler — mixing them directly confuses one thing with another.**

It's like two countries each printing banknotes called "dollars" but with different exchange rates: you can't spend them together without converting.

## The solution: equating (or linking)

The craft of putting items from different batches on the same ruler is called **equating** (also linking). Its central mechanism is **anchor items** (mentioned in article 2).

**Put simply**: have the two batches **share some identical items** (these shared items are the "anchor items").

- Group A has answered these anchor items, and so has group B.
- Because they're the same items, they should have the same parameters.
- So by comparing how the anchor items behave in group A and in group B, we can work out the "exchange rate" between the two rulers (a shift and a rescaling).
- Using this exchange rate, we **convert** the parameters of next year's batch onto this year's ruler.

In this way, items from every batch are brought onto one ruler, and only then is the item bank truly "universal". **Anchor items are the bridge between batches; equating is the procedure for crossing it.**

## Anchor items aren't chosen at random

Whether equating succeeds depends largely on how well the anchor items are chosen. A few principles (it's enough to know them):

- **Enough of them**: with too few anchors the bridge is unstable and the "exchange rate" can't be computed accurately.
- **Good quality**: anchor items must themselves be good items (high discrimination, good fit); bad anchors pull the whole bridge out of line.
- **Representative**: the anchors' difficulties should cover a wide range — not all easy or all hard.
- **Stable, without drift**: anchor items should behave consistently across batches. If an anchor "drifts" (perhaps it has leaked or been predicted), it's no longer dependable and must be dropped from the anchor set (parameter drift, from article 27).

**In one sentence**: anchor items are what hold the item bank steady; choose them badly and the whole bank's comparability collapses.

## An item bank is "alive" and needs ongoing care

Building an item bank isn't the end; it's the beginning. It needs long-term maintenance:

- **Adding new items**: items age and leak, so fresh blood must be added continually (each new batch equated into the bank).
- **Retiring old items**: items that are over-exposed or have drifted should be retired promptly.
- **Regular check-ups**: periodically recheck whether parameters are still stable and whether new DIF has appeared.

So a good item bank is **an asset built up year by year and continually cared for, growing in value the more it's looked after**. That's exactly where the long-term value of this kind of service lies (articles 7 and 26) — not a one-off sale but infrastructure that can serve a client for many years.

## In one sentence

> **A real item bank is a collection of items whose parameters are stable and comparable on one ruler, and only IRT can build one. The craft of bringing items from different batches onto one ruler is called equating; it uses anchor items as a bridge to compute the "exchange rate" between batches. Anchor items must be numerous enough, good enough, representative, and free of drift. An item bank is an asset that is cared for year after year and grows in value.**
