# A08 · Developing a Scale from Start to Finish: From Construct Definition to Full Administration

> In-depth companion to Q05. Can be split into a Xiaohongshu card series, "Eight steps of scale development".

## Scale development is engineering, not writing

"Writing a questionnaire" sounds like a writing task, but it is really measurement engineering: requirements analysis (defining the construct), a prototype (the item pool), review (content validity), testing (the pilot), iteration (psychometric item selection), acceptance (CFA and reliability), and documentation (the user manual). This article walks through the whole process, giving the deliverable and the typical way things go wrong at each step.

## Step 1: Define the construct — 90% of failures are decided here

Deliverable: a one-page **construct specification** containing the theoretical definition (one paragraph), the dimensions and the rationale for them, examples of how each dimension shows up in behaviour, and a **boundary statement** (what the construct does not include, and how it differs from neighbouring constructs).

How it goes wrong: skipping the definition and going straight to writing items. The consequences surface two months later, when the factor analysis produces dimensions that don't match what you had in mind — because you never had anything clearly in mind. **The test: can you explain to a layperson in two minutes what you are measuring and what you are not?**

## Step 2: The item pool — produce to a blueprint, and over-produce

Allocate items to dimensions and write 2–3 times as many as you will need (expect half of them not to survive). Items come from three sources: established scales (mind the permissions and translation standards), the actual words of people from the target population collected in interviews (the best source of good items), and expert experience.

Item-writing discipline: one idea per item ("the content is interesting and useful" is two items); avoid negative wording, especially double negatives; match the language to the population's reading level (a scale for primary-school children should read like something they would say); and use reverse-worded items sparingly (the cost of the method effect usually outweighs the benefit of deterring careless responding; beginner articles 37 and 46 show how reverse-worded items cause trouble in analysis).

## Step 3: Content review and cognitive interviews — two quality checks before any data

**Expert review**: 5–8 subject experts rate every item (relevance, clarity, representativeness); compute the CVI and decide item by item whether to keep, revise, or delete. Ask the experts to review fairness at the same time: do the scenarios in the items favour the experience of a particular region, sex, or social class?

**Cognitive interviews**: 8–15 people from the target population complete the questionnaire while thinking aloud: "How did you understand this item? Why did you choose that answer?" What you meant as "using spare moments to learn" may be understood as "slacking off at work". Only interviews catch misunderstandings like this; no statistic can.

## Step 4: The pilot — your first look at real data

With 100–300 respondents, look at four things: the response distribution (if 90% choose the same option, the item carries no discriminating information and is a candidate for deletion); item–total correlations (flag items below 0.3); a first α per dimension; and open feedback (which items people couldn't understand). This round removes the obviously weak items, and the item count converges towards the final version.

## Step 5: EFA — let the structure emerge

Run an exploratory factor analysis on a new sample (or on the pilot sample, if it is large enough):

- Decide the number of factors with parallel analysis (`psych::fa.parallel`), not just the eigenvalue > 1 rule.
- Use principal axis or ML extraction with an oblique rotation (the dimensions of a construct are almost always correlated).
- Deletion rules: primary loading < 0.40, cross-loadings (difference < 0.20), very low communality.
- **Delete one item at a time and rerun after each deletion**; the factor structure shifts as items are removed.
- If the emerging structure doesn't match the theoretical blueprint, go back to the construct specification and think it through before acting. Data-driven "renaming of dimensions" requires theoretical awareness; it is not a matter of casually naming whatever factors appear.

## Step 6: CFA + reliability — acceptance on an independent sample

**You must use an independent sample** (EFA and CFA on the same data is circular reasoning, and a frequent reason scale papers are rejected). Test the structure established by the EFA: fit indices (CFI/TLI, RMSEA, SRMR), standardised loadings, and correlations between dimensions (very high correlations suggest redundant dimensions). Report ω/α for each dimension as well.

For extra credit: measurement invariance (configural → metric → scalar across sex or grade) — only once it has been established are you entitled to compare group means; and IRT item analysis (GRM/GPCM for polytomous items), using item information curves to check each dimension's precision in the target ability range and replacing low-information items. **This is where the process for an academic scale parts ways with that for a formal assessment instrument** — the latter has to pass both the IRT and the DIF checks.

## Step 7: Criteria and norms

Evidence of relations to external variables (convergent, discriminant, and criterion-related — see the long article on validity). If the scale is to produce "high/medium/low" judgements, you need a norm sample (the score distribution of a representative reference group) or a standard-setting procedure to set the cut-offs. **Without norms, "a score above X indicates high anxiety" has no basis.**

## Step 8: The user manual — from "a set of items" to "an instrument"

Contents: construct definition, intended population and settings, administration instructions, scoring rules (including reverse-scored items and missing data), a summary of reliability and validity evidence, norm tables or the basis for cut-offs, and how to cite the scale. The manual is what allows others to use and cite the scale correctly, and it is the complete form of the scale as a scholarly output.

## An honest budget of time and samples

The full process needs 2–4 independent samples (pilot, EFA, CFA, and criterion/norm studies can be partly combined) and takes from six months to two years. The realistic simplification for a thesis is to combine the pilot and the EFA and run the CFA on a split half of the sample — the bottom line is that **the EFA and the CFA must not use the same data**. Simplify any further and you are betting that your reviewers don't know the field.

Scale development is the "general surgery" of psychological and educational measurement, and every step has its own specialist literature. Later articles in this series will go into key steps (IRT item selection, measurement invariance, standard setting) — stay tuned.
