# 25 · A Quick-Reference Card for Abbreviations: θ, a, b, c, ICC, TIF, and IIF Once and for All

> The finale of the terminology section. This article is a "pocket crib sheet": a single sweep through the symbols and abbreviations you meet most often in IRT, each with a plain-language explanation and a memory hook. If anything is unclear, go back to the article listed and review it.

## Three parameters for items and people (Greek and lower-case letters)

| Symbol | Name | In plain language | Memory hook | See |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **θ** | theta | A person's **ability/level**, usually between −3 and +3 | θ looks like a 0 with a line through it — "where a person sits on the ruler" | 04, 14 |
| **b** | difficulty | How **hard** an item is (the level at which the chance of a correct answer is 50%) | b = the bar to clear: the larger the b, the harder the item | 03, 09 |
| **a** | discrimination | How well an item **tells people apart** (how steep its curve is) | a comes first — the most important; steep = high a | 03, 10 |
| **c** | guessing | The baseline chance of **getting it right by luck** without knowing anything | c = chance; the small step at the left end of the curve | 11 |

**Putting it together in one sentence**: for a person of ability **θ** answering an item with difficulty **b**, discrimination **a**, and guessing rate **c**, these four determine the probability of a correct answer.

## Three common curves (capital-letter abbreviations)

| Abbreviation | Full name | In plain language | What it looks like | See |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ICC** | Item Characteristic Curve | The curve showing "the higher the level, the easier it is to get it right" for a single item | S-shaped | 08 |
| **IIF** | Item Information Function | A single item's "sweet-spot map": the people it measures precisely | A small hill | 12 |
| **TIF** | Test Information Function | The information of the whole test added up: which people the whole test is good at measuring | A mountain (possibly with several peaks) | 13 |

**How they relate**: transforming the ICC gives the IIF (a single item's sweet spot); adding up all the IIFs gives the TIF (what the whole test can do). The ICC is the root; the other two are branches and leaves.

## Some model names

| Abbreviation | Full name / alias | In one sentence | See |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1PL / Rasch** | One-parameter model | Difficulty b only; the simplest and most robust | 06 |
| **2PL** | Two-parameter model | Difficulty b + discrimination a; common in the social sciences | 06 |
| **3PL** | Three-parameter model | Adds guessing c; suited to multiple-choice items | 06 |
| **GRM** | Graded Response Model | Handles multi-category Likert items such as "strongly agree …" | 06 |
| **PCM/GPCM** | (Generalized) Partial Credit Model | Handles items scored "by steps" | 06 |
| **MIRT** | Multidimensional IRT | Measures several things at once (several θs) | 17 |
| **CAT** | Computerised adaptive testing | A harder item after a right answer, an easier one after a wrong answer | 28 |

## Some quality and statistical concepts

| Term | In plain language | See |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Latent variable** | Something invisible that can be measured indirectly (θ is one) | 16 |
| **Unidimensionality** | A set of items measures only one thing (a precondition of classic IRT) | 17 |
| **Local independence** | At the same level, items have no extra links between them (no colluding) | 18 |
| **Parameter estimation** | Working back from right/wrong data to the parameters (turning the dials for the best fit) | 19 |
| **Maximum likelihood** | Choosing the parameters that "best explain the data" (the detective picks the scenario) | 20 |
| **Standard error (SE)** | The error range of an estimate (the more information, the smaller) | 21 |
| **Goodness of fit** | Whether the model and the data fit each other (the mirror of truth) | 22 |
| **DIF** | Whether an item favours some group once levels are equalised (fairness) | 23 |
| **Reliability** | Whether a measurement is stable and dependable (a curve in IRT) | 24 |
| **Validity** | Whether a measurement is right — whether it measures what it's supposed to | 24 |
| **Equating** | Joining different tests onto one ruler so scores are comparable | 02, 44 |

## Three golden rules for reading results (a review)

1. **Whenever you see an estimate, ask first about its standard error** — a number without an error range is cheating (article 21).
2. **Before you calculate anything, confirm unidimensionality + local independence** — on shaky foundations, the taller the building, the more dangerous it is (articles 17 and 18).
3. **Never skip the fit check** — it's the mirror that shows up problems; skipping it is what's really dangerous (article 22).

## How to use this card

Treat it as a dictionary. From now on, whenever you read an IRT report or sit through an analysis presentation and meet a symbol or abbreviation you don't know, look it up here for a plain-language explanation, then go to the corresponding article for the details. Look things up a few times and the jargon becomes part of your everyday vocabulary.

**The only difficulty with technical terms is unfamiliarity. Once you've met them a few times, they're not scary at all.**
