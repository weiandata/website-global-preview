# A21 · A Quality-Control System for Survey Data: The Whole Chain from Design to Publication

> Assessment practice track. A systematic long-form article for project managers, with high business value.

## Quality isn't inspected in

Manufacturing learned this a century ago, yet the survey industry often forgets it: **quality is a product of design and process; inspection at the end can intercept defects but cannot create quality.** If quality control on a survey of hundreds of thousands only begins "after the data come back", all that is left is amputation. This article sets out a QC framework covering the whole life cycle — an operational version of the Total Survey Error framework.

## Phase 1: Design — closing off sources of error one by one

**Defences against measurement error**: construct definition → expert review → cognitive interviews → pilot, each step with its own deliverable (see the article on scale development). **Save a week here and you will pay a month later.**

**Defences against coverage and sampling error**: check that the sampling frame is complete (people who are not on the frame can never be selected — a coverage error no weighting can repair); calculate whether the sampling plan matches the target precision (plan for the smallest reporting unit — see the article on sample size); **record the selection probability at every stage on the spot** — it is the raw material for the weights.

**Built-in quality checks**: instructed-response items, contradictory pairs, timestamps — detection tools must be in place before the questionnaire is finalised.

**Freezing the plan**: write the cleaning rules, exclusion criteria, weighting approach, and core analyses into a "data processing plan" and have it reviewed and approved before the data arrive. It is both an institutional barrier against p-hacking and the blueprint for a team working together.

## Phase 2: Fieldwork — monitor in real time instead of being surprised afterwards

The biggest benefit of electronic data collection is that **process data are visible in real time**. Use them:

- **Monitor both progress and composition**: look not only at the number of returns but at their composition (quota completion for each stratum or unit). If one type of unit is falling systematically behind, intervene at once rather than forcing the numbers into line with weighting afterwards.
- **A daily quality report**: the distribution of completion times, straight-lining rates, and logical-conflict rates for each day's new cases, broken down by site or interviewer. Deal with anomalous clusters (one interviewer's questionnaires take 3 minutes on average) the same day.
- **Field verification**: call back a proportion of respondents to verify (were they really interviewed? are the answers to key items consistent?). This is an iron rule of household surveys, and online surveys need their own version (device fingerprinting, detecting clusters of IP addresses).
- **The iron rule for mid-fieldwork revisions**: even a one-character change must be logged as a new version, with the mapping between old and new codes updated at the same time. Uncontrolled questionnaire revisions are among the leading causes of data disasters in large projects.

## Phase 3: Processing — six cleaning steps + weighting + a full audit trail

The six cleaning steps (structural checks → quality screening → missing-data triage → coding and derived variables → external consistency → cleaning report) and the three stages of weighting (design weights → non-response adjustment → calibration) were covered in earlier articles. From a system perspective, three additions:

1. **Independent double computation**: key components (weights, core indicators) are implemented independently by two people and the results compared. It isn't cheap, but it is worth it on formal projects — the errors it catches are the most expensive ones.
2. **Code as documentation**: script the whole workflow under version control, and generate the "data processing notes" from the scripts rather than writing them by hand (handwritten documentation inevitably drifts away from the actual code).
3. **Assertions on intermediate outputs**: every step's output comes with automated checks (sample sizes conserved, weights summing to the population total, proportions within [0, 1]), and any failed assertion stops the line. Every processing program we deliver to clients has such assertions built in — when the program runs in the client's environment, the assertions are our remote eyes.

## Phase 4: Analysis and publication — the last gate

- **Auditing consistency of definitions**: every number in the report can be traced to exactly one generating script; check that the technical report and the press release use the same definitions (θ scale vs proportion correct, weighted vs unweighted). Conflicting definitions are the classic script for public challenges after publication.
- **A final sweep for extreme values**: run an order-of-magnitude sanity check over every summary table (one province's mean is 3 standard deviations above its neighbours'? Check the data before announcing a finding).
- **A sensitivity appendix**: show how robust the key conclusions are under different cleaning rules or weighting settings. Writing this up proactively is far more dignified than patching things up after being challenged.
- **A reproducible archive**: package and archive the frozen data version + code + environment manifest + technical documentation. The goal: an audit three years later can rerun everything with one command.

## A health check

Score your project (1 point each):

1. A written data processing plan exists and predates the data
2. Selection probabilities are recorded at every stage
3. Fieldwork has daily quality reports and records of interventions
4. Cleaning rules are frozen before analysis
5. Raw data are read-only and the whole workflow is scripted
6. The weights have complete technical documentation
7. Key figures are computed twice independently
8. Every number in the report can be traced back in one step
9. There is a sensitivity analysis
10. The whole project is archived reproducibly

8 points or more is a serious project; for projects scoring below 5, treat the conclusions as for internal reference only. We have refined this system over many years of data processing on large national surveys. There is nothing mysterious about it — the hard part is genuinely doing every item.
