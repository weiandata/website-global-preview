# 26 · IRT's Three Great Strengths, One Sentence Each

> We've praised IRT here and there along the way. This article pulls out its three core strengths and explains each in a single sentence, so you can tell a client "where IRT's strength lies" in three sentences.

## Opening: why summarise the strengths at all?

Because sooner or later you'll have to explain to someone — your boss, a client, a colleague — "why we're spending extra to use IRT". What you need then isn't a pile of detail but **three sentences that hit the nail on the head**.

IRT has many strengths, but only three really hold it up. Remember them and you've grasped the soul of IRT.

## Strength 1: comparability across tests — "different tests, one ruler"

**In one sentence**: IRT can join tests from different years and with different items onto the same ruler, so that scores are genuinely comparable.

Ordinary scoring can't do this, yet countless situations demand it. As we've seen, total scores are stuck to their tests and can't be compared once the test changes. Yet in reality:

- large monitoring programmes change their items every year but must answer "has there been improvement since last year?";
- an exam produces several forms to prevent cheating but must ensure "people who take form A and form B are treated fairly";
- if you want an item bank you can reuse, items must be comparable across batches.

IRT uses "separating people from items + bridging with anchor items" (equating) to pin these tests to the same scale. **This is the lifeblood of every project that tracks change over time, uses multiple forms, or runs an item bank.** Without it, such projects simply can't stand.

## Strength 2: checkable precision — "it tells you honestly where it's precise and where it isn't"

**In one sentence**: IRT doesn't pretend to be equally precise for everyone; it gives you a complete, explicit map of "which people it measures precisely and which it measures poorly".

Ordinary scoring gives one reliability coefficient and acts as if "I'm precise for everyone". IRT uses the information function and the standard error to draw precision as a curve that changes with level (articles 13, 21, and 24).

That honesty is enormously useful in practice:

- Need to make high-stakes decisions at a pass mark? IRT can prove that "we measure precisely enough near that line".
- Some group isn't measured precisely? IRT can pinpoint exactly which levels, and guide you to add items there.
- Reports can state truthfully that "this test is most dependable for people of medium level", without misleading clients.

**Knowing where you're imprecise is far more professional, and far more dependable, than pretending to be precise everywhere.**

## Strength 3: manageable items — "every item has an ID card and can become an asset"

**In one sentence**: IRT gives each item stable parameters (difficulty, discrimination, fairness), so items can be managed in detail, reused over the long term, and built into an item bank.

With ordinary scoring, an item's "difficulty" changes with the population; once the test is over, it's spent and nothing is retained. IRT gives every item a "file" that is stable across populations, so you can:

- build an **item bank**: reuse items and assemble tests to order (article 44);
- run **adaptive tests**: select items in real time according to performance (article 28);
- carry out **fairness checks**: test each item for DIF and remove biased ones (article 23);
- **keep improving**: retire bad items, strengthen weak areas, and make the bank better with use.

**Items turn from "single-use consumables" into "assets that grow in value"** — that's the long-term value IRT brings to assessment organisations.

## Put the three sentences together and you have an elevator pitch

> "IRT's strength lies in three things. First, it makes scores from tests of different years and different forms **fairly comparable**. Second, it **tells you honestly** which people a test measures precisely and which it doesn't, instead of pretending to be precise everywhere. Third, it gives each item a stable 'ID card', so items can **build up into a reusable item bank**. Comparability across tests, checkable precision, manageable items — that's why we spend extra on it."

Memorise that, and you can calmly handle any "so what's actually good about IRT?" question.

## An honest addendum

None of these three strengths comes free or holds unconditionally — they rest on the model's assumptions being met and the sample being adequate (articles 29 and 30 pour cold water on things). **The strengths only pay off when IRT is used correctly.** Someone who knows the field doesn't overpraise IRT — and that's exactly where their credibility comes from.

## In one sentence

> **IRT's three great strengths: comparability across tests (one ruler for different tests), checkable precision (it says honestly where it's precise and where it isn't), and manageable items (every item has an ID card; item banks become possible). Memorise these three sentences and you have the perfect elevator pitch — but remember, they all depend on IRT being used correctly.**
